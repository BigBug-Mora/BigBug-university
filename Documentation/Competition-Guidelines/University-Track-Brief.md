# AI Catalyst ’26 University Track brief

Source [Innodata AI Catalyst event brief](Innodata_AI_Catalyst.pdf), pp. 2–8. See the [submission guide](University-Submission-Guide.md) for repository paths and file specifications.

## Challenge and schedule

Teams of four eligible university participants choose a current global problem in one of eight domains. The domains are AI and Robotic Engineering, Cybersecurity, Manufacturing, Logistics, Healthcare, Telecommunications, Agriculture, and Pharmaceuticals. Eligibility covers third- and fourth-year undergraduates and fresh graduates.

The build phase runs 16–29 November 2026, and evaluation runs 30 November–6 December. The ten university finalists advance to the 7 December finale at Hilton Colombo Residences, Colombo 02, from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. Sri Lanka time. Phase 1 contributes 40 marks, carried into the 60-mark final round.

## Research and evaluation

1. Justify the problem with independent research. Identify who is affected, gaps in current approaches, and why action is timely.
2. Quantify the global impact with credible sources across the relevant economic, social, environmental or humanitarian dimensions.
3. Define the mitigation strategy, including scope, users, objectives, outcomes, assumptions and constraints.
4. Design at least two AI agents with distinct responsibilities that exchange information and use each other’s outputs. Show orchestration, shared state, decision-making, conflict resolution, and human oversight in the design, and demonstrate real interactions through execution logs or traces.
5. Compare the multi-agent system, a comparable single agent, and human performance on the same documented tasks. Report quality, completion time and cost with test conditions, sample sizes and limitations. Consider scalability and robustness where relevant.
6. Document metrics, datasets or scenarios, test methods, and limitations covering bias, safety, privacy and reliability. Supply reproducible setup instructions, source, an architecture diagram, technology choices, logs and benchmark results. Disclose reused components, mocks, prerecorded outputs and incomplete features.

The proposal is due 20 November at 11:59 p.m. The complete project plan and runnable MVP are due 29 November at 11:59 p.m. The plan covers architecture, agent design, benchmarks, evaluation, milestones and risks. The MVP must demonstrate its core multi-agent workflow and single-agent comparison.

The finale awards 10 marks each for problem relevance and value, working product and functionality, final-day feature development, benchmarking and comparative results, reliability and practical feasibility, and presentation and technical defence.
